## THE FINDING

**Jade's failure mode is the opposite of the one the industry tests for.**

The probe pack was engineered to catch hallucination. Jade does not hallucinate — it passed both traps and refused an uncomputable question with an honest mechanical explanation. Its actual failure is **false negatives: it reports "no data" about data the user's own device recorded.**

Asked for HRV — which this ring captured **352,441 times** — Jade says the values are not available.

### Mechanism: a lossy document pipeline between sensor and AI

Every user question lands in one of three buckets:

| Bucket | Jade's response | Correct? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Covered by a pre-built document | Confident, specific, numeric | ✅ Yes |
| 2. Absent from documents, **present in raw data** | "No data" | ❌ **False negative — the damaging case** |
| 3. Not computable from anything | "No data" + reason | ✅ Yes (good behaviour) |

**Buckets 2 and 3 are indistinguishable to the user.** Both say "no data." So a user cannot tell "your ring never recorded this" from "I can't see what your ring recorded" — and will reasonably conclude their device failed to capture data it captured perfectly well.

### Why this matters commercially

Jade is free — no subscription revenue attached — so it must earn its cost through retention and differentiation against Oura's paid membership. A layer marketed as "the world's first real-time biointelligence AI" that cannot see 352k HRV readings is not gimmick-by-hallucination; it is **capability-bounded by an ingestion pipeline, and the bound is undisclosed.** The fix is an engineering problem (widen document coverage, or give the model query access), not a model-quality problem — which is the actionable recommendation.
